# Regular-compact note — drafting instructions

A compact note is the short editorial frame that appears beneath the bare
title of a Regular Agenda item. It is the smallest unit of analysis in The
Agenda — one sentence that explains what the item actually does, in plain
language.

## Length budget

- **text** — one sentence, 60–180 characters. Plain text. No emphasis
  markers. Ends with a period.

## What to write

- Translate procedural language into the substantive thing being decided.
- Mention the counterparty or constituency when it's load-bearing.
- If a dollar figure makes the item meaningful, include it.

## What to avoid

- Don't restate the title. The item's name renders right above your text.
- Don't editorialize. Describe, don't evaluate.
- Don't reference the Board's role generically ("the Board will consider").
  Get to the substance.
